Why people love it

It points you to the exact spot

Precision Finding turns your iPhone into a little compass, with an arrow, distance readout and haptic taps that guide you to the tag. The new Ultra Wideband chip extends how far away that guidance kicks in. Engadget saw the "getting closer" screen appear at around 80 feet, and Tom's Guide held a connection to roughly 74 feet, both well beyond the first generation. In practice, that means you can start the search from the hallway instead of tearing apart every room.

You can actually hear it now

Apple says the new chime is 50% louder, and testers agree it's easier to pick out. Macworld measured about 85 dB versus 66 dB for the original tag, and the higher-pitched tone cuts through background noise. That matters when keys have slipped between couch cushions or a bag is buried under coats, and commenters on the MacRumors forums point out it's a real help for people with some hearing loss.

Your wrist can do the searching

For the first time, Precision Finding works from an Apple Watch (Series 9, Ultra 2 or newer). Left your phone in the other room and can't find your car keys? Raise your wrist, open Find Items and follow the arrow. Both Macworld and Tom's Guide called this one of the most useful upgrades.

Built for travel days

Slip one tag into checked luggage and another into a backpack, and the Find My network keeps reporting their location as nearby Apple devices pass by. If an airline misplaces a bag, Share Item Location lets you send a temporary link to the airline's team. The link stops working once you recover the item, when you end it, or after seven days.

Set it and forget it

Setup is a single tap with your iPhone, and there's nothing to charge each night. The CR2032 coin cell comes pre-installed and is easy to swap, and reviewers expect about a year between changes. Find My warns you when a battery runs low.

How to get the most out of it

  • Tag your top four. Popular picks from reviewers and forum users are keys, a backpack or purse, checked luggage and the car, so you can find it in a big parking lot.
  • Add the Find Items control to your Apple Watch. Edit the watch's Control Center and add it, so Precision Finding is one tap away.
  • Share household tags. You can share an item with up to five people, which suits family car keys or a shared suitcase.
  • Turn on Lost Mode if something goes missing. Anyone who finds the tag can tap it with an NFC-capable phone to see the contact details you choose to share.
  • Keep a spare CR2032 in a drawer. Swapping it takes seconds: just twist off the back cover.

FAQ

Does the new AirTag work with Android phones?

No. You need an Apple Account and an iPhone or iPad to set up and track an AirTag. Android phones can detect an unknown AirTag traveling with them, but they can't use it as their own tracker.

Will my old AirTag key ring fit the 2nd generation?

Yes. The new AirTag has the same 31.9 mm diameter and 8 mm thickness as the original, so holders and loops made for the first generation fit.

What battery does it use, and how long does it last?

It uses a standard, user-replaceable CR2032 coin cell that comes pre-installed. Apple says battery life varies with use, and reviewers expect roughly a year between swaps.

Is the AirTag waterproof?

It's rated IP67, meaning it's built to handle a maximum depth of 1 meter for up to 30 minutes. Apple notes that resistance can decrease with normal wear, so it's not meant for swimming.
