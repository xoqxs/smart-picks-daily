Why people love it

It digs deep into carpet

This is where the Navigator really earns its reputation. Vacuum Wars credits its stiff brushroll bristles and strong airflow for pulling grit out of deep carpet, and TechGearLab found it especially good on low-pile rugs. Run it over the living room rug after a busy weekend and you'll be surprised what comes up into the clear dust cup.

One vacuum, two jobs

Press the release, lift the pod off the floor nozzle and you're carrying a compact canister with a hose attached. TechGearLab, Vacuum Wars and Reviewed all point to this for stairs, upholstery and hard-to-reach spots. That means cobwebs in ceiling corners, crumbs between couch cushions and dust on the stair treads, all without hauling out a second machine.

Dust stays inside, not in the air

Shark pairs a HEPA filter with its Complete Seal Technology so fine dust gets trapped instead of puffing back out of the vacuum. TechGearLab rated its fine-particle filtration as excellent, and Vacuum Wars noted how uncommon a sealed system is in this class. It's a real plus in homes with pets or lots of dust.

Easy to steer around furniture

Swivel steering lets the head turn with a twist of your wrist, so you can curve around table legs and chair bases instead of backing up and starting over. Reviewers at TechGearLab and Reviewed found it easy to push and turn, and Vacuum Wars said it felt lighter on its feet than most uprights. Cleaning a crowded dining room goes a lot faster.

How to get the most out of it

  • Flip the brushroll off on bare floors. Vacuum Wars recommends it for hard surfaces. Just note that TechGearLab found fine sand harder to pick up in that mode, so a second slow pass helps in entryways.
  • Use the hose for big debris. TechGearLab and Vacuum Wars both found larger bits like cereal or popcorn can get pushed ahead of the floor head. Grab the hose or crevice tool for those and save the nozzle for everyday dirt.
  • Check the brushroll for hair. TechGearLab and Reviewed both saw long hair wrap around the brush. Snipping it free every so often keeps suction strong and the brush spinning freely.
  • Keep the filters and screen clean. The HEPA and foam filters are washable, and Vacuum Wars and TechGearLab both tie steady suction to keeping filters and the screen clear. Rinse with plain water and let them dry completely before you put them back.
  • Lift away for the stairs. Rather than balancing a full upright on a step, take the pod off and carry it, as TechGearLab and Vacuum Wars suggest. It's the easiest way to do a staircase top to bottom.

FAQ

Does the Shark Navigator NV352 have a HEPA filter?

Yes. Shark lists a washable HEPA filter plus foam filters, paired with its Complete Seal Technology, which is designed to keep captured dust inside the vacuum.

Can you use the Shark NV352 on hardwood floors?

Yes. A brushroll shutoff lets you turn off the spinning brush for bare floors. Reviewers found it does its best work on carpet, so expect a second pass for fine grit on hard floors.

Is the Shark Navigator NV352 good for pet hair?

It handles everyday pet hair on carpet and has a sealed HEPA system, but long hair can wrap around the brushroll, so plan to clear it now and then. For homes with several heavy shedders, TechGearLab suggests looking at Shark's Rotator models.

What does Lift-Away mean on a Shark vacuum?

Lift-Away means the canister pod detaches from the floor nozzle so you can carry it with the hose and attachments to clean stairs, furniture and other spots above the floor.
