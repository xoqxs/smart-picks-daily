Who it suits (and who should look at a sibling)

The original Power Grip is the pick if your skin feels tight under makeup or your base tends to look flat by the afternoon: the gel adds moisture and a fresh, dewy look. If shine is your main complaint, e.l.f. sells a Power Grip Matte Primer with a lemon myrtle blend aimed at excess oil. And if you want a brightening angle, there's a Power Grip Primer + 4% Niacinamide. Same grip idea, different finish, so pick by the result you want at 4 p.m., not by the label.

What actually makes it work

The tack. Right after you apply it, the gel stays slightly sticky. That surface is what foundation and concealer cling to, which is the whole point of a "gripping" primer.

Hydration first. Hyaluronic acid is a moisture-binding ingredient, so the primer doubles as a light hydrating layer before makeup.

No color. The gel looks blue in the tube but goes on clear, so it won't shift your foundation shade.

The one habit that changes the result

e.l.f.'s own tip is to pat it in with your fingertips instead of rubbing, because the warmth of your fingers helps the gel melt into skin and spread evenly. Use a small amount: too much and the tack turns into pilling when you add foundation. Give it a short moment to settle so it feels tacky rather than wet, then apply your base.

Quick checklist

  • Want dewy, longer-lasting makeup? Original Power Grip.
  • Fighting shine? Look at the Matte version instead.
  • Pat, don't rub, and use a thin layer to avoid pilling.
