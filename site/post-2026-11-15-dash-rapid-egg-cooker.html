Who gets the most out of it

This is a meal-prep tool. If you eat eggs most mornings, pack egg salad for lunch, or keep peeled eggs in the fridge as a snack, cooking seven at once saves real stovetop time. It's also handy where a stove is awkward to use, like a small apartment or an office kitchenette. If you eat an egg once a week, a saucepan is still fine.

How it decides when eggs are done

Water sets the result. Dash includes a measuring cup that tells you exactly how much water to add for the eggs you want, so there's no timer to set.

The sensor does the rest. Dash says the precision thermal sensor alerts you when the eggs reach the doneness you chose, which is what keeps them from overcooking.

Three trays, three jobs. Dash includes a 7-egg boiling tray, a poaching tray for two eggs and an omelette bowl, and they all store inside the cooker.

One thing to do every time

Use the pin on the bottom of the measuring cup to pierce each egg before boiling; that's what it's there for. Then pay attention to the water line you choose, since that, not a dial, sets your doneness. Once you find the mark you like, you'll get the same egg every time.

Quick checklist

  • Cook eggs several times a week? This saves time.
  • Pierce eggs with the cup's built-in pin first.
  • Pick your water mark once, then repeat it.
