How to get the most out of it

  • Pair it with a USB-C cable that's up to the job. The charger doesn't come with a cable. Use a USB-C to USB-C cable (or USB-C to Lightning for older iPhones) to get full fast-charging speed.
  • Use it for one device at a time. With a single port, it's at its best fast-charging your main device. A multi-port charger makes more sense for a crowded nightstand.
  • Think of it as a laptop top-up charger. Reviewers found it keeps a MacBook Air going while you work but won't charge it as fast as a higher-watt charger. That makes it a great travel backup, not a replacement for a laptop's main charger.
  • Expect some warmth under heavy load. CNN Underscored notes it gets a little warm at full output. That's normal for a charger this small, and ActiveShield 2.0 monitors the temperature.

Is the Anker Nano 30W the same as the Anker 511 Nano 3?

Yes. Anker originally sold it as the Anker 511 Charger (Nano 3, 30W) and now calls it the Anker Nano Charger (30W). It's the same single-port 30W USB-C charger with folding prongs.

Can the Anker Nano 30W charge a MacBook Air?

Yes, at a slower pace. Digital Camera World kept an M2 MacBook Air powered through editing work, and CNN Underscored says it suits thin-and-light laptops. It won't charge a laptop as fast as a higher-watt charger.

Does the Anker Nano 30W come with a cable?

No. The standard charger is sold without a cable, so you'll need a USB-C cable that fits your device.

How fast does it charge an iPhone?

Digital Camera World measured an iPhone 14 Pro going from about 1% to 50% in just over 30 minutes, with a full charge in a little over an hour and a half.
