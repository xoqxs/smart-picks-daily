How to get the most out of it

  • Plug straight into a wall outlet. TechRadar found the width can crowd neighboring sockets on a power strip, so wall outlets are the tidiest home for it.
  • Pick devices with a "stay on" switch. Lamps, fans, string lights and drip coffee makers that resume when power returns work best. Gadgets that need a button press after power-up won't turn on by themselves.
  • Keep the setup code handy. Snap a photo of the Matter code on each plug before you install it, so adding it to a second platform later, like Apple Home alongside Alexa, is quick.
  • Use 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. The plug connects on 2.4 GHz only, so make sure that band is enabled on your router before you start.
  • Name plugs by room. Labels like "Bedroom Lamp" or "Tree Lights" make voice commands natural and keep your app organized as you add more devices.

FAQ

Does the Tapo P125M work with Alexa?

Yes. It works with Amazon Alexa as well as Apple Home, Google Home and Samsung SmartThings, thanks to Matter certification. No separate hub is required for basic Wi-Fi control.

Does the Tapo P125M need 5 GHz Wi-Fi?

No. It connects on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only and uses Bluetooth during setup, so make sure your router's 2.4 GHz band is turned on.

Can the Tapo P125M monitor energy use?

No. It handles on/off control, schedules, Auto-Off and Away Mode. For per-plug energy readings, choose an energy-monitoring model such as the Tapo P110M.

What can I plug into it?

It supports up to 15 amps and 1800 watts, and TP-Link markets it for lamps, fans and higher-power appliances like kettles, coffee makers and toasters. Devices that switch back on by themselves when power returns work best.
