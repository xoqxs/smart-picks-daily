How to get the most out of it

  • Fill water just below the valve. Bialetti, America's Test Kitchen and seasoned owners all say to keep the water line under the safety valve in the bottom chamber.
  • Use a medium-fine grind. Aim for something a little coarser than espresso. Grind that's too fine can clog the flow and taste harsh, a point Bialetti and moka pot fans make repeatedly.
  • Don't tamp the coffee. Fill the funnel basket to the top, level it off with a finger, and leave it loose. Packing it down slows the brew and makes it bitter.
  • Keep the heat low to medium. Gentle heat gives you a smoother cup. Many owners start with hot water from the kettle to shorten the time the coffee sits on the burner.
  • Pull it off at the gurgle. When the coffee starts to sputter, take the pot off the heat. America's Test Kitchen and moka pot regulars agree this is the moment to stop.
  • Rinse, don't scrub. Bialetti recommends washing with water only and never in the dishwasher. Let the parts dry fully before reassembling.

FAQ

Does the Bialetti Moka Express make real espresso?

It makes strong, concentrated, espresso-style coffee using steam pressure on the stove. It's not the same as machine-pulled espresso, but reviewers consistently describe it as the closest stovetop option.

Does the Moka Express work on an induction cooktop?

Not on its own. Bialetti says it works on gas and electric stoves, and on induction only with its induction adapter plate.

How much coffee does the 6-cup Moka Express make?

About 9.1 fluid ounces per brew, based on Bialetti's US capacity chart. Each "cup" is a small espresso-style serving.

Can I put the Moka Express in the dishwasher?

No. Bialetti recommends rinsing it with water only and letting it dry before you put it back together.
