How to get the most out of it

  • Aim for the thickest part. Slide the probe into the center of the thickest section and keep it away from bone and fat, a tip both the manual and The Grilling Dad stress.
  • Give it a moment to settle. Hold the probe still for a few seconds until the number stops climbing, then hit lock and read it away from the heat.
  • Know the USDA numbers. USDA guidance lists 145°F (with a 3-minute rest) for whole cuts of beef, pork, veal and lamb, 160°F for ground meats and 165°F for all poultry. A thermometer makes hitting them simple.
  • Check it in ice water now and then. A glass of ice water should read 32°F. If it drifts, use the built-in calibration mode.
  • Rinse, don't soak. The IP65 rating handles a rinse under the faucet, but reviewers and the manual both note it isn't made to be submerged. Keep a small Phillips screwdriver handy for battery swaps.
