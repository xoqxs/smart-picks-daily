How to get the most out of it

  • Wet the grounds slowly. Food Network's writer pours water slowly over the grounds, and America's Test Kitchen found that fully wetting the coffee is key to a rich, full-bodied brew. Pour in stages and give the grounds a moment to soak.
  • Plan on 12 to 24 hours. Both America's Test Kitchen and Bob Vila note that cold brew typically steeps 12 to 24 hours. Start it after dinner and it's ready for the next day's commute.
  • Dial in your strength. Food Network's writer uses about eight tablespoons and a roughly 14-hour steep for a milder cup; add more coffee or time if you like it bolder.
  • Dilute to taste. Treat the result as a concentrate. America's Test Kitchen cites Takeya's suggested 1:1 mix with water, and you can stretch it further with milk or ice.
  • Keep it upright and the lid snug. CNN Underscored and America's Test Kitchen both note it can leak if tipped or if the lid threads aren't lined up, so store it standing in the fridge door.
