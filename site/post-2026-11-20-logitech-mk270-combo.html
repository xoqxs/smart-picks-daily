How to get the most out of it

  • Grab an adapter for newer laptops. The receiver uses USB-A. AppleInsider points out that modern MacBooks need a USB-C adapter or dock, and TechGearLab also lists the USB-A dongle, so check your ports before it arrives.
  • Store the receiver in the mouse. TechGearLab notes the receiver tucks inside the mouse, which keeps it from getting lost when you pack up.
  • Customize the mouse with Logitech software. PCWorld explains that the middle button can be remapped through Logitech's Options app, for shortcuts like locking the screen or opening a program.
  • Know the Mac quirks. The keys follow a Windows layout. PCWorld and AppleInsider both say it works on a Mac, but expect to relearn where Command and Option sit, and the hot keys can't be customized there.
  • Pick your typing angle. The tilt legs offer two positions, flat or 8°. Try both, and add a separate wrist rest if you type for long stretches, since TechGearLab found the keyboard sits fairly tall.

FAQ

Does the Logitech MK270 work with a Mac?

Logitech officially lists Windows and ChromeOS, but AppleInsider and PCWorld both report that it works with a Mac. The keys use a Windows layout and the hot keys can't be customized on macOS, and newer MacBooks need a USB-C adapter for the receiver.

How long do the Logitech MK270 batteries last?

Logitech rates the keyboard for up to 36 months on two AAA batteries and the mouse for up to 12 months on one AA battery. Real-world life depends on how much you use them.

Do the keyboard and mouse need separate receivers?

No. Both connect through one small USB receiver, so you only use a single USB-A port, and the receiver can be stored inside the mouse when you travel.

Is the MK270 good for gaming?

It's designed for everyday office and home use. Reviewers describe it as a budget productivity combo, so dedicated gamers will be happier with a gaming keyboard and mouse.
