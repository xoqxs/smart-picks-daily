Why people love it

It hooks right onto your keys

Many trackers need a separate case or keychain before they're useful. The SmartTag2's large metal loop is part of the design, so it goes straight onto a key ring, a backpack zipper or a suitcase handle. Samsung built the ring shape for exactly this, and Android Authority singles out the hook as a real everyday convenience compared with tags that need an extra holder.

Finding things up close feels almost magic

On a UWB-capable Galaxy phone, SmartThings Find can show on-screen arrows with direction and distance, and an AR mode uses the camera to guide you right to the item. Samsung describes these as Compass View and AR Find, and Android Authority found the directional guidance impressively accurate. That's a big help when the keys are buried in a coat pocket in a crowded closet.

Set it and forget it for a long time

Samsung rates the tag for up to 500 days on one CR2032 coin cell, and up to 700 days with Power Saving Mode turned on. Tom's Guide and Android Authority both point to battery life as a headline upgrade over earlier SmartTags, and Samsung Community members who travel often say the same. When it finally runs low, the battery is replaceable, so the tag keeps going.

A button that does double duty

The single button is more than a ringer. Tom's Guide and Android Authority both describe pressing the tag to make your phone ring when the phone is the thing that's lost, and you can map presses to SmartThings actions, such as switching lights on or off. One Samsung Community member calls the automation button their favorite feature, which makes the SmartTag2 a tiny smart-home remote too.

Peace of mind if it goes missing

If someone finds your keys, Lost Mode lets them tap the tag with any NFC-capable phone to see the contact details and message you saved in SmartThings, regardless of whether they use Android or iPhone, according to Samsung and Tom's Guide. Add IP67 water and dust resistance, and the tag is ready for rainy commutes, gym bags and checked luggage.

How to get the most out of it

  • Fill in Lost Mode before you need it. Samsung notes your contact info has to be registered in SmartThings ahead of time, so set it up the day you pair the tag.
  • Turn on Power Saving Mode for luggage. For a suitcase that sits in a closet between trips, Samsung's Power Saving Mode stretches battery life toward the 700-day mark.
  • Program the button. Tom's Guide and Android Authority both use it to control smart devices; try a single press for hallway lights when you walk in.
  • Use it to find your phone. Samsung Community users and Tom's Guide both mention pressing the tag to make a misplaced phone ring.
  • Check the privacy settings. Android Authority suggests switching on location encryption and unknown-tag alerts in SmartThings, since they aren't always on by default.

FAQ

Does the Galaxy SmartTag2 work with iPhone?

No. It pairs with Samsung Galaxy phones and tablets through the SmartThings app. The only exception is Lost Mode, which lets anyone with an NFC-capable phone, iPhone included, see your contact details if they find the tag.

How long does the SmartTag2 battery last?

Samsung rates it for up to 500 days in normal mode and up to 700 days in Power Saving Mode. It uses a replaceable CR2032 coin battery, so you can swap in a fresh one.

Is the SmartTag2 waterproof?

It is rated IP67 for water and dust resistance, so rain and splashes are fine. Samsung advises against taking it to the beach or pool.

What is the range of the SmartTag2?

Samsung lists a Bluetooth range of up to 120 m. When the tag is farther away, SmartThings Find uses nearby Galaxy devices to report its location.
