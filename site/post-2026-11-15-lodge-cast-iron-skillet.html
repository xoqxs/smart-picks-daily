Who should buy the 10.25-inch size

This is the "everyday" size: big enough for two steaks, a pan of cornbread or four fried eggs, small enough to lift with one hand plus the assist handle. Cooking for a family of four or more most nights? Look at the 12-inch version instead. Cooking for one? This size still makes sense because it fits in most ovens and sinks.

Why cast iron still wins

Searing. A thick pan stores heat, so it doesn't cool down as much when cold food hits it. That's the difference between a browned crust and gray meat.

Oven jobs. No plastic handle means you can sear on the stove and finish in the oven, bake a skillet cookie, or roast vegetables.

No coating to scratch. The seasoning is baked-on oil, not a chemical layer, and you can rebuild it at home if it ever gets patchy.

One thing to watch: the handle

The whole pan, handle included, gets hot. Keep an oven mitt or a silicone handle cover nearby every time. For care, wash by hand, dry right away, and wipe a thin layer of cooking oil on the surface before storing it. That's all the "maintenance" most people need.

Quick checklist

  • Cooking for 1–3 people? 10.25 inches is the sweet spot.
  • Always grab a mitt: the handle heats up.
  • Dry it fully and oil it lightly after washing.
